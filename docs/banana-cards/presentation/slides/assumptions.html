I audited my own assumptions

5 stated up front, 14 surfaced on review, each marked accepted, mitigated or open.

Resolved by design

Presence survives trading? Now it lives in keepsakes.

Suspense without rarity? Equal odds from a visible pool.

Quick joiners are adults? Accounts are 13+.

The UI stops a market? Accepted: we don’t police it.

Still open

Remote fans have someone to trade with.

A digital card feels like a keepsake, and outlasts its service.

More fans is good while tickets are scarce.

Player pay tracks audience growth. Needs a source.

Banana Cards · 12

As a thought experiment, this is built on assumptions rather than research, so I made them explicit. The left column shows four that changed the design: the pool and keepsake split, equal-odds pools, the 13+ decision, and dropping the idea of policing a market. The right column is what I’d test or source next. A ticketless fan with no local fans can collect but never trade. A physical card outlives its maker; a digital one doesn’t. Growth without seats could grow frustration unless the payoff is streams, merch or new markets. And the social-drop business case rests on player pay being tied to audience growth, which I haven’t sourced.
