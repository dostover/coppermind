Two kinds of cards

How you earned it decides whether you can trade it

Pool cards

From a general code: a random card from a visible pool, every card equally likely.

Tradeable. Value from sets, story and the swap.

Keepsakes

From a specific code: one card, because of something you did.

Never traded. Presence stays with the fan who was there.

Pools start around 24 cards per team per season: too big to finish alone, easy to finish by trading with your section. No tiers, no “rare” labels.

Banana Cards · 6

This came out of auditing my own assumptions. If a “you were there” card can be traded, presence ends up with people who weren’t there. And a reveal needs surprise, but surprise usually brings rarity back. The answer: two kinds of cards. Pool cards come from general codes, drawn when you scan from a visible pool with equal odds, and they trade freely. Keepsakes come from a specific code, like the one in a player’s hat when you stay for an autograph, and they never move. A keepsake can be scarce, but because it never trades, scarcity never becomes its value: scarce like a signed ball, not like a chase card. Rough math: a 24-card pool takes about 90 pulls to finish alone; a section of 50 fans turns up most of it.
